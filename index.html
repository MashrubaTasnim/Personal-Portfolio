About me

Hello! I'm Mashruba Tasnem Oishi, a Computer Science & Engineering graduate of IUBAT. My undergraduate thesis focused on Explainable AI — building a leakage-controlled, statistically validated (Holm-corrected McNemar, Nadeau–Bengio t-tests) network intrusion detection framework on UNSW-NB15 and CIC-IDS-2017, benchmarking Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost classifiers with SHAP and LIME explainability. The manuscript is currently submitted to Annals of Data Science (Springer Nature).

I recently completed a Web Developer Internship at SoftDeft, where I designed and built a full-stack Animal Rescue & Sanctuary Management System with role-based portals, secure CRUD operations, and AI-assisted features.

Fueled by my passion for AI, cybersecurity, and software development, I enjoy turning complex ideas into practical, user-centered solutions.

"I aim to use technology for the betterment of mankind, animals, and the environment."

Creating impactful systems that promote safety, kindness, and sustainability. I'm also actively pursuing opportunities to teach core Computer Science courses and mentor undergraduate researchers.

Outside the world of technology, I am an animal enthusiast, artist, and lifelong learner who loves painting, reading, and exploring creative ideas.

02
XAI-NIDS: Explainable Intrusion Detection

Leakage-controlled, statistically validated ML framework for network intrusion detection. Achieved 90.52% accuracy / 90.43% F1 / 98.37% ROC-AUC with XGBoost on UNSW-NB15, with 9/10 pairwise model comparisons significant under Holm-corrected McNemar testing. SHAP and LIME explainability, independently validated on CIC-IDS-2017, plus a temporal (day-based) evaluation exposing generalization limits beyond standard random-split testing. Manuscript submitted to Annals of Data Science (Springer Nature).
